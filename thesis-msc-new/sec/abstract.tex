High Performance Computing (HPC) is the use of supercomputers and parallel processing techniques to solve
computationally demanding problems.

HPC has traditionally relied on specialized on-premises infrastructures, built around dedicated supercomputers
and tightly coupled networks, to meet the demands of intensive computational tasks. However, the advent and
rapid evolution of Cloud technologies now offer a flexible, scalable, and cost-effective alternative for
deploying HPC applications. As a result, leading organizations in academia and across industry are
increasingly replacing or supplementing their traditional HPC systems with Cloud-based solutions.

This thesis explores HPC in the Cloud, focusing on AWS ParallelCluster as a platform for running HPC workloads
efficiently. The study adopts the OSU Micro-Benchmarks and Algebraic Multi-Grid (AMG) solvers as representative
workloads and evaluates their performance experimentally across a range of cluster configurations.

The research contributes to a broader understanding of the role of Cloud computing in the future of HPC and
provides a comprehensive set of best practices that help practitioners get the most out of AWS ParallelCluster
when running HPC workloads.
